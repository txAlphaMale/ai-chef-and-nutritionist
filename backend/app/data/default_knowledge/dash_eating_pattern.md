# DASH Eating Plan -- Summary Reference

**Source:** National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI), part of the
U.S. National Institutes of Health (NIH). Official page:
https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/dash-eating-plan (last updated by NHLBI
February 25, 2026, retrieved for this bundle August 1, 2026).

## What DASH is

DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) is a flexible, no-special-
foods eating pattern developed and validated through NIH-funded research,
built around daily and weekly food-group targets rather than a rigid meal
plan. NHLBI names it "Best Heart-Healthy Diet" and "Best Diet for High
Blood Pressure" in independent 2025 rankings, and it is widely recommended
alongside the Mediterranean and Portfolio patterns for LDL cholesterol and
cardiovascular risk management, not blood pressure alone.

## Core recommendations

- Eat vegetables, fruits, and whole grains.
- Include fat-free or low-fat dairy products, fish, poultry, beans, nuts,
  and vegetable oils.
- Limit foods high in saturated fat: fatty meats, full-fat dairy, and
  tropical oils (coconut, palm kernel, palm).
- Limit sugar-sweetened beverages and sweets.
- Favor foods low in saturated and trans fat; rich in potassium, calcium,
  magnesium, fiber, and protein; and lower in sodium.

## Daily/weekly serving targets (2,000 calorie/day reference diet)

| Food group | Servings |
|---|---|
| Grains | 6-8 daily |
| Meats, poultry, and fish | 6 or less daily |
| Vegetables | 4-5 daily |
| Fruit | 4-5 daily |
| Low-fat or fat-free dairy products | 2-3 daily |
| Fats and oils | 2-3 daily |
| Sodium | 2,300 mg daily (1,500 mg lowers blood pressure further) |
| Nuts, seeds, dry beans, and peas | 4-5 weekly |
| Sweets | 5 or less weekly |

Serving counts scale with a household member's actual calorie needs, not
just the 2,000-calorie reference point above -- NHLBI publishes separate
worksheets for 1,200, 1,400-1,600, 1,800-2,000, and 2,600 calorie/day
levels (see the source page for links) for anyone adapting this pattern
more precisely.

## Relevance to this app's design

- The **sodium ceiling matches the 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines'** own
  2,300 mg/day figure (see the companion `dietary_guidelines_2025_2030.md`
  file in this bundle), so a meal plan built to satisfy one is
  automatically consistent with the other.
- The **weekly "5 or less" sweets allowance** is a concrete, sourced
  number this app's "occasional indulgence" framing can be checked
  against, rather than left as a vague once-a-week impression.
- The **saturated-fat-source exclusion list** (fatty meats, full-fat
  dairy, tropical oils) gives generation-time reasoning specific
  ingredients to deprioritize for a household whose stated goal is
  reducing LDL cholesterol, beyond just a generic "healthy" instruction.
- DASH's **explicit low-fat/fat-free dairy emphasis** is worth noting
  against the 2025-2030 DGA's separate shift toward full-fat dairy (see
  the companion file) -- the two sources genuinely differ here. This app
  does not silently resolve that tension; a household weighing blood-
  pressure/LDL goals (where DASH's low-fat dairy guidance is the more
  targeted, single-purpose source) against general dietary guidance
  should treat this as a real, sourced disagreement between two credible
  federal references, not an error in either document.
