# The Portfolio Diet for LDL Cholesterol Reduction -- Summary Reference

**Sources (peer-reviewed research, not a government publication):**
- Jenkins DJA et al., "A dietary portfolio approach to cholesterol
  reduction: combined effects of plant sterols, vegetable proteins, and
  viscous fibers in hypercholesterolemia," *Metabolism*, 2002. PubMed:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12489074/
- Chiavaroli L et al., "Portfolio Diet Score and Risk of Cardiovascular
  Disease: Findings From 3 Prospective Cohort Studies," *Circulation*,
  2024. https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.123.065551
- Multicentre effectiveness trial (330 participants, 6 months): 13.8% LDL-C
  reduction under free-living conditions. See ClinicalTrials.gov study
  NCT00438425 ("Portfolio 5") and related published results.
- Real-world 1-year adherence data reporting a 13% LDL-C reduction under
  free-living (non-metabolically-controlled) conditions.

**License/provenance note:** Unlike the companion DGA/DASH/FDA/NIAID files

## What the Portfolio diet is

A plant-based dietary pattern combining four cholesterol-lowering
components that produce a larger combined ("portfolio") effect than any
one alone:

1. **Viscous (soluble) fiber** -- oats, barley, psyllium, eggplant, okra,
   and legumes. Binds bile acids and cholesterol in the gut, promoting
   their excretion and lowering circulating LDL.
2. **Plant sterols/stanols** -- naturally present in small amounts in
   vegetable oils, nuts, and seeds, and added to some fortified foods
   (certain margarines, orange juice). Compete with dietary cholesterol
   for absorption in the small intestine, reducing how much reaches the
   bloodstream.
3. **Soy protein** -- tofu, soy milk, edamame, and other soy foods used in
   place of some animal protein.
4. **Tree nuts** -- almonds, walnuts, and similar, in place of
   less-healthy fat sources.

## Reported effect size

Under tightly controlled metabolic-ward conditions, the original Jenkins
trials found LDL-C reductions comparable to a first-generation statin
(roughly 30%). Under realistic, free-living conditions -- the more
relevant comparison for a household actually cooking and eating this way
-- effectiveness trials report smaller but still clinically meaningful
reductions: about 13-14% LDL-C reduction over 6 months to 1 year of real-
world adherence. The gap between controlled-trial and free-living results
is expected and worth stating plainly to a household: full adherence to
all four components, every day, is what the larger effect sizes assume.

## Relevance to this app's design

- This is the dietary pattern most specifically engineered for LDL
  reduction, as opposed to DASH (primarily blood-pressure-focused, though
  it overlaps) or a generic "eat healthier" instruction -- directly
  responsive to a household whose stated goal is reducing LDL cholesterol
  without weight loss being the primary driver.
- The four components translate into concrete, checkable recipe/ingredient
  choices a meal-plan generator can bias toward: include oats/barley/
  legumes and a serving of nuts through the week, prefer a soy-protein
  meal over a red-meat one on some days, and consider a sterol-fortified
  product where the household already uses that product category (e.g. a
  fortified margarine) rather than treating sterol supplementation as a
  requirement.
- This is naturally compatible with a **gluten-free/celiac-safe**
  household: oats used for viscous fiber must be explicitly certified
  gluten-free oats (see the companion allergen/cross-contact material in
  this app's own `allergen_service.py` and the NIAID file in this bundle
  for why "oats" and "gluten-free oats" are not interchangeable), and
  psyllium, legumes, soy, and nuts are naturally gluten-free.
- As with any dietary-pattern grounding material in this bundle, this app
  never asserts a specific numeric cholesterol outcome for a specific
  person -- individual response varies, and any household tracking
  cholesterol trends (this app's health-metrics feature) should discuss
  target LDL levels and the appropriateness of this pattern with their own
  healthcare provider, particularly if already on cholesterol-lowering
  medication.
